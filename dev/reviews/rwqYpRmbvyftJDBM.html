Illidan: from Flames to 20.5%

Flames Healing & landing Later phases The best pull Every pull Next raid

8 September 2026 · Raid 1 · Warcraft Logs report

Nine Illidan attempts, nine wipes, three clears of the air phase, and a best result of 20.52% remaining. This is meaningful progress from September 7, when all three Illidan attempts ended during the first air phase. The next gains come from keeping people alive through the Flames, arriving at the landing with healing assignments intact, and preparing the later-phase positioning and tank cooldowns.

The report is titled “BT / Hyjal,” but its nine boss pulls are all Illidan. There are about 40 minutes of boss combat. Attempt numbers below are chronological; Warcraft Logs skips fight 7 because it is a trash segment. All timestamps are elapsed from the relevant pull. Damage figures exclude overkill unless explicitly stated.

This review uses the deaths from each individual pull, incoming-damage events, healing received, casts, and aura changes. The 224 death-table entries include wipe cleanup, repeat deaths after resurrection, and Spirit of Redemption bookkeeping. They are not 224 independent mistakes. The analysis below follows the first losses and the subsequent collapse; the log alone cannot establish voice calls, assignments, or why a player moved.

Flames: control the facing and leave the fire

Two early attempts lost large parts of the raid to Flame Blast while both Flame tanks were still alive. These deserve more attention than the much larger pile of melee deaths after the tanks fell.

  • Attempt 1, 2:20–2:23: Roarwind’s Flame switched to Badabuille, who died at 2:20.9. At 2:22.8, that Flame’s cone hit 13 players and killed five immediately. Roarwind was still alive. Roarwind had used Growl at 2:14.7, and Badabuille continued attacking through the target change. This is strong evidence of a threat/control problem followed by a disastrous facing change; the events do not establish the exact threat values or positions. Attempt 1 deaths.
  • Attempt 2, 2:35–3:12: Virtuálskill and Badabuille died to repeated Blaze ticks at 2:35.8 and 2:41.8. One Flame died at 2:50, but the surviving Flame then hit eight players with Flame Blast at 3:09.1. Caeliya and Paramedicin took lethal damage in that hit; several more players died to Fireballs within seconds. Neither Flame tank had died yet. Paramedicin’s death-table entry is delayed by Spirit of Redemption. Attempt 2 deaths.
  • Attempt 4, 2:29–2:37: Roarwind accumulated Blaze, residual Demon Fire, Flame Blast, and melee damage before dying at 2:37.2. Healing was landing, including HoTs and a shield, but no direct heal landed after 2:32.8. The final seconds combined floor damage with heavy melee. His form disappeared at death, which does not establish the earlier night’s premature Bear Form cancellation. Attempt 4 deaths.

Next raid: give each Flame tank a clear route, keep its frontal cone away from the raid throughout turns, and have melee stop attacking when threat or footing becomes unsafe. Move off Blaze promptly; do not trade a death for a few extra swings. Keep Flames near their glaives and use separated ranged groups, roughly 11 yards apart, to limit Fireball splash. These positioning constraints are described in the Illidan encounter guide.

Eye Blast still ended two attempts: Jonette was the first death at 1:58.1 on attempt 5, and Roarwind died to it at 2:34.3 on attempt 9, after Kalinak had already died. Assign one beam-direction caller and rehearse the tanks’ escape routes. The five simultaneous beam deaths on attempt 6 happened after Jonette had fallen; they were additional losses, not that pull’s opening failure.

Healing: Barrage needs an owner, and landing needs a handoff

Dark Barrage repeatedly went without enough immediate healing. Its sustained single-target damage needs a named responder and backup, especially when the target is already tanking. The ability deals approximately 3,000 shadow damage per second for ten seconds. Mechanic reference.

On attempt 6, Jonette was tanking the remaining Flame when Barrage began around 3:19. He also took Blaze and melee damage. His last health-restoring heal landed at 3:18.6; a Power Word: Shield absorbed 2,412 at 3:21.8, but he died to melee moments later. The response must cover the Barrage without dropping the underlying tank assignment. Attempt 6 deaths.

On the best pull, attempt 8, Shcherbaque received no effective healing during the Barrage that killed him at 3:23.3. Bruand died to a later Barrage at 4:10.1; his only healing during that channel was his own 1,979 Holy Shock. The channel began just after the second Flame died. Keep the Barrage assignment active through the transition. Losing healers here makes every later mechanic harder even if the raid survives the air phase. Best-pull deaths.

On attempt 7, both Flames were dead by 4:02.6, but Madef died at 4:23.8, shortly after Illidan landed. In his final eight seconds, only 1,975 effective healing landed: two Regrowth ticks and one Earth Shield heal. Four melee hits in the final four seconds removed 20,589 health, with further overkill on the last. This is a clear gap in healing received; the log does not reveal whether range, movement, lost healers, or assignment confusion caused it. Attempt 7 deaths.

Next raid: explicitly call “ground-tank healers back on Madef” before the landing, with a named backup if either assigned healer is dead or moving. Keep existing Barrage targets covered until their debuff ends. Tank healers should agree who can move and who is casting during Flame repositioning, rather than discovering the gap after a death.

Later phases: spread before the damage arrives

The air-phase grouping must end before the raid resumes the ground fight. On attempt 3, the Agonizing Flames cast at 4:38.5 applied the debuff to five players together: Bruand, Klib, Magicmamba, Gahenna, and Aleksov. On attempt 8, the cast at 4:48.4 applied it to 11 players. That creates prolonged healing pressure from a single positioning error. The later periodic ticks should not be counted as eleven new mistakes every five seconds.

Pre-spread for Agonizing Flames, whose initial hit affects players within five yards. Do this before the cast rather than waiting for the debuff warning. The encounter’s spacing requirements are covered in the mechanic guide.

The first demon phase on attempt 3 exposed a separate positioning problem. At 5:36.5, the first Shadow Blast hit seven players. Klib took 2,520 after mitigation, while the other six took roughly 5,400–10,900 each. Klib was alive and receiving the intended attack: the raid was also in the blast area. Another blast dealt lethal damage to Paramedicin at 5:39.8, while Agonizing Flames and Flame Burst contributed further losses. Attempt 3 damage taken.

Mark Klib’s demon-tank position and clear its 20-yard Shadow Blast splash radius before the transformation finishes. Give melee an explicit retreat destination that does not overlap Klib or the rest of the raid. Maintain personal spacing for Flame Burst and assign ranged players to kill Shadow Demons promptly. Demon-phase mechanics.

Seven Consume Soul deaths followed on that attempt, but several people had already fallen. Fixing the earlier spread and splash failures preserves the players needed to handle the demons. Demon targeting still needs an assignment; the death total alone does not measure how good that assignment was.

One additional avoidable loss on the best pull: Gahenna died to his own Shadow Word: Death at 5:29.2, less than a second after taking 3,583 from Agonizing Flames. The recoil removed his remaining 2,526 health and overkilled him by 482. Avoid that cast when a periodic tick has left insufficient health for its recoil.

The best pull: prepare the Frenzy response

Attempt 8 reached Maiev’s phase, then lost Madef 4.6 seconds into Frenzy. Shadow Prison marked the phase transition at 6:01.2. Earlier deaths had already reduced the raid, but this tank death was the decisive final collapse.

Illidan gained Frenzy at 7:18.5. At 7:20.2, two melee hits removed 14,380 health. Madef used Shield Block at 7:21.5, and multiple healers were healing him. At 7:23.1, another pair of melee hits removed 17,534 health, plus 863 overkill. He died immediately afterward. No Shield Wall, Last Stand, or successful Caged effect was recorded in this pull. This was not the same healing gap as attempt 7: healing was arriving, but the burst still killed him. Best-pull damage taken.

Agree the response before reaching 30%: identify a usable Maiev trap, call the movement early, and reserve a major tank cooldown for an uncaged Frenzy. Illidan’s final-phase enrage increases damage and attack speed; Maiev’s traps can stop him during it. Shield Block remains useful, but did not provide enough protection here. Final-phase mechanics.

Also review the boss target at 6:47.4, when Klib died to Illidan’s melee while Madef was alive. Klib’s Hand of Protection had expired five seconds earlier. This warrants a threat and positioning check; it does not prove that the protection’s expiration caused the target switch. Set a clear threat check when returning to normal attacks and before protection expires.

There are positives to retain: no Shear debuff was recorded on Madef across the nine attempts, and Draw Soul damage was confined to Madef before the tank losses. The large multi-player Draw Soul hits on attempts 7 and 8 came after those collapses. They are not evidence that routine boss facing was the main problem all night.

Next raid: five calls to agree before pulling

  1. Flame movement and threat: each tank names the route; one player calls beams; melee stop when the Flame turns or footing becomes unsafe.
  2. Barrage target and backup healer: call the name immediately, with extra coverage for a tank target. Continue healing the channel through the landing.
  3. Landing handoff: confirm Madef’s healers before the second Flame dies and replace any dead assigned healer.
  4. Spread and demon positions: move out of the air-phase groups before Agonizing Flames, mark Klib’s clear area, and name the Shadow Demon damage assignments.
  5. Frenzy plan: name the trap caller and reserve Madef’s major defensive response before entering Maiev’s phase.

Between boss attempts, the median gap was about 5:24. Keep recovery parallel: one person gives a short cause-and-fix call while resurrection, rebuffing, and reassignment happen. Judge the next run by fewer losses before the landing and successful later-phase transitions, rather than by raw death totals or damage rankings on pulls already lost.
